**Figure 14.** *(provisional caption — figure and wording still to be tweaked.)* **One global state, a local direction everywhere.** **(A)** The attentional state is a single global quantity — *attend the counter-clockwise sense*, the large arrow — but each point-set receives it as a **local** preferred direction, tangential to its own position. 116 of the 1473 point-sets are drawn, at a constant 0.33° spacing both radially and along each ring, each snapped to a real point-set. Three examples are circled at their FWHM/2 and numbered.

**(B)** The same global state read at those three point-sets, as a bias across the eight direction channels: bump = exp(2 cos(θ − d)), min–max normalised per point-set. The **▼** marks each one's preferred direction. Because the preference is tangential, it tracks polar angle: point-set **1** at polar angle 0° prefers **88°**, **2** at 120° prefers **211°**, **3** at 240° prefers **329°**. Three different channels carry the bias, from one unchanged global state.

**The bias is global in extent and amplitude, and fixed for the trial — it is not a surface tag.** Nothing in it names a surface. It becomes surface-specific only because the two fields **counter-rotate**: at any point-set their local directions are opposite, so a bias toward one sense favours whichever field happens to be moving that way there.
