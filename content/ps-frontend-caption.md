**Figure 9.** *(draft caption — figure and wording still to be tweaked.)* **The front end — the common input to every model that follows.** Drawn on the **right-hand V1 receptive field of [Figure 8](#figure-8)**, at a **single frame** of rotation: the drive is computed frame by frame, so only the dot's instantaneous direction is shown and no path is drawn. **(1)** One dot inside the receptive field, which is drawn at Figure 8's radius of 0.24°, read here as FWHM/2 — equivalent to a Gaussian σ of 0.204°, just above the tiled model's own 0.08–0.19° range. The **spatial weighting profile** is plotted beneath, in full and on the receptive field's own spatial scale — the axis is in degrees and the panel is the same width as the disc above it, so the curve's half-height falls directly under the circle's edge: a dot at the centre weighs 1.0 and one at the edge exactly 0.5 — which is what FWHM/2 means, and is why the circle is drawn where it is. This dot sits 46% of the way out, so *W*<sub>g</sub> = 0.87. **One dot is the typical case rather than a simplification** — measured on the replication stimulus, 51% of receptive fields contain no dot at all, 29% contain exactly one, and only 20% contain two or more. **(2)** That dot then meets two tuned weights, one on direction and one on hue. Each hypercolumn has eight channels with von Mises tuning at κ = 2 (all eight ghosted; the one the dot drives is solid). Direction takes every value across the display, so all eight direction channels are used; hue only ever takes two, red and green, which is why only those two are named and the remaining six carry a swatch of the hue they prefer. A receptive field holding both surfaces simply sums two such lobes. **(3)** The raw drive is the product of the two weights — with one dot, a single term, and the only place stimulus *strength* enters the model at all. Neighbouring channels receive 55% of the peak, the ±90° channels 12%, and the opposite channel exactly zero. **(4)** Each stream is then divided by its own channel sum — motion and colour separately, so neither can divide the other down. Written out, the normalized drive is *R*<sub>i,k</sub> = γ·*D*<sub>i,k</sub> / (σ + Σ<sub>j</sub>*D*<sub>i,j</sub>) — the canonical divisive-normalization form of Carandini & Heeger at an exponent of *n* = 1, with the *i* carried into the sum because each point-set is divided by its own total and not a global one. The figure uses the literature's symbols; in the code *D* and *R* are both `ff` (it is overwritten in place), γ is `ffScale` and σ is `ffFloor`. The gain **γ = 3** is what the eight channels are collectively rescaled *to*: once the sum is appreciable the total comes out at 3 whatever the raw drive was, so each channel ends up carrying its *share* rather than its magnitude. ⚠️ It is a bare constant in the model's parameter struct with no derivation on record. **σ = 1**, the semi-saturation constant — the drive at which the rescaling reaches half strength — is a floor, and it is the only thing standing between a nearly empty receptive field and being rescaled to full strength — with it set near zero, as it was until July, an empty receptive field was as active as an occupied one. Together they pin the total: over a 67-fold change in dot density the summed drive moves by a factor of 1.09. **(5)** Only then do the models begin. Bars are drawn in a single ink throughout — colour is used only where it denotes the actual stimulus.
