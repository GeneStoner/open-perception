**Figure 18.** *(provisional caption — figure and wording still to be tweaked.)* **A rotation bias becomes a translation bias.** **(A)** Both channels grow with the cue lead — 16 layouts, MT read-out, unselected, τ_E 20 ms. The bias acts on **rotation** directly and needs no transfer; **translation** is reached only via the pool. Both peak (translation at 80 ms) and then fall, because the dots move on: the pool is charged at a *place*, so it is **displacement** that erodes it, not time.

**(B) The ratio is what carries the argument.** Rotation needs no transfer, so settling depresses both channels and divides out; a **rising** ratio is transfer being established, whereas a flat one would be settling alone. It rises **0.192 → 0.272**, at plateau by 20 ms. Beyond that it is flat within noise — the largest excursion is 0.044 ± 0.027, only 1.62 SE.

**(C) Where each channel carries it** — 12 layouts, 750 ms lead, no swap. Translation runs **0.058 → 0.144 → 0.011** across PRE, TRANS and POST; rotation barely moves.

**The mechanism.** The bias up-weights the **pool afferent** for dots moving in the locally-cued rotational direction (see the bias-map figure). The pool charges and returns a **scalar** gain that multiplies *every* channel of that point-set. When the field stops rotating and translates, that scalar is still there and multiplies the 0° channel. **The gain does not know the direction changed — that is the transfer.**

⛔ **The argument is the ratio in B, not an onset.** At τ_E 20 ms the first 10 ms frame already gives a third of plateau, so "the effect is absent at short leads" is not available. At the superseded τ_E 150 ms operating point it was.
