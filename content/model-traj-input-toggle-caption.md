**Figure 3.** **Feature trajectories ⟷ what the model sees.** Toggle (or let it alternate) between two views of the same four conditions (A–D, after Stoner & Blanc, 2010, Fig. 4). Within the object-based framework, "cued" indicates that the delayed dot field translates and "uncued" indicates that the non-delayed (first-on) dot field translates. Here we look at a single MT hypercolumn whose collective receptive field lies on the *right side* of the rotating display: within this RF, clockwise rotation is locally **downward** motion, counter-clockwise rotation is locally **upward** motion. The brief translation, in this example, is rightward. Thus the motion signals within this collective RF are: Downward, Rightward, and Upward. 

**Feature trajectories:** each field's motion direction over time with *line style* indicating field identity (dotted = the field that briefly translates) and vertical jumps marking transitions. The gray band marks the 40 ms translation; the brief onset offset (≈ 750 ms) is the delayed onset of the second field. Line color would indicate the dot field color but for these next modeling exercises, we ignore color (color differences are not necessary for the cueing effect; [Mitchell, Stoner, & Reynolds, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02584)).

**What the model sees:** the same feature trajectories with dot-field identity discarded — every line now black, solid, indicating simply the motion directions present in the MT RF. This object-neutral depiction is what we would expect area MT neurons to “see” and what we feed into our model.  With this depiction, you see that the cued no-motion-swap condition is identical to the uncued motion-swap condition. Similarly, the uncued no-motion-swap condition is identical to the cued motion-swap condition.  Hence, if it's just the motion signals present in the RF (independent of object identity) that determines the cueing effect, the motion-swap manipulation should reverse the cueing effect.  [Stoner & Blanc (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.11.015) tested that prediction and found that the cueing effect did NOT reverse: the cueing effect was specific to object (dot field) identity.
